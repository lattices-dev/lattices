What the lens does

  • The frame is the anchor. The tray, the aperture and its corner guides keep their geometry from 0 to 1. Only the contents change scale: the strip's track shrinks inside the aperture until every desktop fits as one 4 × 4 miniature workspace (a regular grid sized for 16 Spaces; fewer desktops use fewer rows and columns).
  • The focus ring comes out of the aperture. At 1:1 the ink ring and the aperture are one shape, as in the native strip. While zooming out, the ring follows the selected desktop down to its cell, and the aperture stays behind as a quieter lens edge.
  • Coral only for where you are. One coral dot marks the desktop that is actually showing. The ink ring is what you would land on. Browsing never moves the coral until you land.
  • The wings are the end cards. At the miniature stop, the tray space either side of the aperture carries the quiet Lattices summary that the end cards show at the first and last desktop. It does not take clicks.
  • Stage 2: the aperture becomes a minimap. Past the miniature stop, the frame keeps its copy of the 4 × 4 map and shrinks into the corner as a persistent minimap. The desktops themselves fly out of it and close up into one continuous workspace plane: adjoining desktop regions on a shared grid, divided by quiet seams, carrying their window zones. You can drag, scroll and pinch it. A viewport rectangle on the minimap shows which part of the canvas is in view, and clicking or dragging the minimap jumps there. In the app this canvas would be Overview's existing canvas; here it is a stand-in.
  • Held arrows keep their speed. A held ←/→ at 1:1 hides every preview detail and shows only the flipping number, as natively. Zoom never starts from a key repeat.

Controls

← → at 1:1 switch desktops right away (the background swaps). When zoomed out, ← → ↑ ↓ move the ring across the map without switching. − zooms out one stop, = or Return zooms in one stop, ↵ lands on the ring's desktop, and esc returns to the desktop you started on. At the miniature stop, clicking a cell lands on it. On the canvas, drag or scroll to pan and pinch to zoom; click a region to move the ring, double-click to land, and click or drag the minimap to jump. The slider scrubs the same curve the buttons play.

The material passes

Lattice glass. The lattice is structure, not texture: a fine grid etched into the tray wings that fades out before it reaches the aperture, and a dot grid on the canvas plane. Edges are lit from above with a single hairline. White corner brackets mark the selection; the coral square marks the desktop you are on, so the two never share a colour or a shape.

Cut glass. Glass takes its colour from the desktop. Ivory edges mark the selection; coral still marks the desktop you are actually on. Square registration points and the small lattice mark give the frame a sharper, architectural character.

In both glass passes, blur is limited to the strip and the small minimap. Window zones stay simple shapes, and the large canvas uses a tinted surface. This browser study does not establish native rendering cost.

What to judge

  • Does the miniature read as "the same strip, further away", or as a new screen?
  • Does the ring leaving the aperture still read as one continuous selection?
  • At the miniature stop, is the 4 × 4 map big enough to be useful inside a frame this size, or is it only a waypoint to stage 2?
  • Does the minimap read as the same aperture, now parked in the corner, rather than a new widget?

Native mapping and cost

  • Stage 1 is one scale-and-translate on the track layer, pivoting on the aperture. The tray, aperture, guides and rail do not change geometry.
  • At the miniature stop, all 16 cards are visible. Today the strip builds only what is near the aperture. When zoomed out, cards should be window rectangles only: no labels, no captures.
  • The strip's additive travel animations must be paused or flattened before the scale starts, or they fight the transform.
  • Trigger: Ctrl plus scroll or a pinch while the strip is up, or a Lattices-owned chord. Bare Ctrl+↓ is macOS App Exposé and should not be the key.
  • Stage 2 adds one small minimap layer and moves the canvas with a single pan/zoom transform. Window zones are rectangles from the same per-space frames; no captures are needed.
  • Reduce Motion: zoom stops crossfade in place (about 120ms) and the strip snaps instead of travelling.

Limitations

Layouts are invented. Timing is a browser approximation of the native curve (0.22, 0, 0, 1), not Core Animation. The stage 2 canvas is an illustration and stands in for Overview, whose real layout and data are unchanged by this concept. Multi-display and full-screen Spaces are not modelled.
