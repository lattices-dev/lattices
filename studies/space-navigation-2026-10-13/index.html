A little joy between desktops.

Three ways to acknowledge an instant switch. Drive them together, then compare what stays legible when you move quickly. These are simulated HUDs; they do not switch your Mac’s desktops.

Your destination stays in the center. Numbered neighbors slide beneath a fixed aperture, like a small instrument.

Keep: a constant footprint, even with many desktops.

Quiet Compass Least distraction

A crisp number, a direction cue, and one tiny position mark. Enough orientation to reassure you, then out of the way.

Keep: attention on the desktop, a readable destination.
Watch: less personality and less precise global position.
Native: text transition plus a five-point marker.

What I’d put beside Opus’s ideas

Viewport is my exploratory pick. Shuttle is the low-risk refinement.

Viewport changes the spatial metaphor while staying cheap to draw. The question for us: does the moving strip feel more anchored, or does seeing the whole rail matter more?

Same rules for every direction

No end-of-line flash, bounce, sound, or “edge” label. Reversals retarget immediately. Reduce Motion removes movement. The real desktop switch never waits for the acknowledgement.
