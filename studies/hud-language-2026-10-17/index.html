One language for the things that appear over your work.

The Stage pill, the layout suggestion card and the space switcher, drawn as one set: ink #101518 at 86%, a thin white keyline, light system type, and coral only where something is live. Every state is drawn and labeled. These are simulated overlays on a simulated desktop; nothing here touches your Mac.

The space switcher is the piece that changes. Today it is the gray filmstrip with a cyan edge. It is shown once as the current app, then restyled in each state.

Stage and the card share one slot

Top centre, 6pt under the visible frame. When Stage is on, the pill holds the slot and no suggestion is offered. When Stage is off, the slot is empty until a suggestion arrives. One owner at a time.

The switcher is its own moment

It answers a keypress you just made, sits where you set it (centre by default), and leaves within a second. It never docks into the top-centre slot, so it never competes with the pill or a pending suggestion.

So the three never stack

The pill and the card are ambient: they wait for you. The switcher is a reply: it follows you. Mixing a waiting thing with a reply in one place makes both harder to read.

What stays the same across the set

One surface (ink 86%, white 12% keyline, 12–14pt radius, the same soft shadow), one window-block style for any map, one accent. Coral marks the thing that is live right now: Stage running, the offer's remaining time, the desktop you are on.
